Design exercise: feeds, fan-out and locality
Part 2 • Synthetic narration

Partitioning a social graph

Draw six members of a discussion service across three storage partitions. Give each member subscriptions to authors on both other partitions. Placing a member's subscription list beside that member helps discover followed authors locally, but it cannot also place every author's canonical posts beside every subscriber without copying them. Moving an author to satisfy one reader may make another reader's path less local. The small graph exposes the conflict before any large-scale benchmark. Compare three different copies. A replica preserves another copy of the same owned data for a defined availability or read-serving purpose. A materialized feed stores a reader-oriented representation of posts. A cache retains reusable results under a freshness policy. All consume resources, but their ownership and update rules differ. State which operation needs each copy and how stale it may become. Hashing member identifiers can spread ownership, yet it does not eliminate popular-author hotspots, cross-partition relationships or network transfer. Scaling claims must include those remaining costs and skewed workloads.

Hybrid feeds and the celebrity exception

An author in a hypothetical community has one hundred thousand followers, but only five thousand of them read during a short freshness window. Eagerly updating every feed pays for one hundred thousand destinations. Fetching the author's recent posts during those active readers' requests may do much less work, especially if results can be shared through a cache. These illustrative counts motivate a hybrid rather than a rule that every post must follow the same publication path. Precompute entries for ordinary authors and merge selected high-fan-out authors at read time. Choose the threshold using observed fan-out cost, active readership, freshness and cache behaviour, not a fixed celebrity label. The merge still needs stable ordering and pagination: a post appearing in both paths must not be shown twice, and deletion must affect both. The storage technology for the exceptional path is a separate decision. This is an architecture exercise about moving work between reads and writes, not a verified description of a current commercial social network.
