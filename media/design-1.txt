Design for extension

The open–closed principle encourages stable code around deliberate extension points. In the shape example, a common drawing operation keeps the painter independent of concrete shape types. This does not eliminate all edits or require inheritance everywhere. Judge the abstraction by the changes it localizes and the contract new implementations must satisfy.

Substitutability and contracts

Liskov substitution preserves the expectations clients are entitled to make. The mutable Rectangle and Square example fails because setters violate independent-dimension behavior. Check actual preconditions, results and invariants. Exceptions are not automatically violations: optional operations may allow them. The right question is whether the subtype honors the contract the client was promised.

Client-focused interfaces and dependency inversion

Design interfaces around client roles and put high-level policy behind appropriate abstractions. Read-only and mutable capabilities need not be bundled for every client. JDBC shows how a stable API can separate application logic from driver details. These boundaries reduce coupling, but only a preserved behavioral contract makes replacement safe.
