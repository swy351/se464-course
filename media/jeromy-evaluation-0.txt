1. Define the decision and its business context — 1. Define the decision and its business context

Quality-Oriented Decision Analysis (QODA) is Jeromy’s lightweight method for making architectural decisions informed, shared and durable, while allowing deliberate revision. A technology choice becomes an architectural decision when it commits a system to consequential tradeoffs. Jeromy’s approach begins by making the context explicit: whose problem is being solved, what outcomes matter, which constraints apply, and what future changes are plausible. A favorite product is not a substitute for this definition.

1. Define the decision and its business context — Apply the idea: Define the decision and its business context

The lecture uses a data-querying example at Datadog, where diverse kinds of data and different storage systems make a unified query experience difficult. Keep that as the motivation for the method, rather than assuming any historical product comparison remains a current recommendation. New worked example used throughout this unit: a campus information service must search course data, export records, survive worker failures, and integrate new data sources. Alternative A is a tailored service; B is a shared platform. Before comparing them, record stakeholders, workload, operating limits, and non-negotiable constraints. The example’s targets and scores are teaching assumptions. Two teams both want a “good platform.” Have they agreed on a decision criterion? No. They may value latency, incident recovery, development effort or cost differently. Name those priorities and the circumstances in which each matters before comparing products.

2. Turn quality goals into observable scenarios — 2. Turn quality goals into observable scenarios

A scenario gives a quality claim a concrete meaning. The lecture uses context, stimulus and response: the conditions in force, the event that occurs, and the result the system must produce. Make the response measurable where possible. A scenario can concern normal execution, failure, or a development change. Facilitate the process with stakeholders. The case describes agreeing on context, inviting participants to brainstorm scenarios, refining vague statements, and then clustering the resulting scenarios into a quality tree. This bottom-up collection helps expose concerns that a preselected taxonomy or a product-led debate might miss.

2. Turn quality goals into observable scenarios — Apply the idea: Turn quality goals into observable scenarios

For the campus example, Search means: at 200 requests/second on a specified representative dataset, a user issues a search and p95 latency is at most 250 ms during a defined test window. Export means: an authorized user requests 10,000 records and receives a complete export within eight seconds. Specify hardware, data size and concurrency in an actual evaluation. Worker loss means: during a request, one serving worker terminates and the request completes correctly within two seconds. New source means: with the platform in production, the team integrates a new catalog source and produces a tested adapter within three engineer-days. These are new scenarios, not copied lecture questions. Check data correctness and authorization as well as timing. Agree on how evidence will be collected for every scenario: a load test, a fault-injection experiment, a prototype integration, or a justified analysis. A threshold without a test context is not yet a reproducible requirement. “The system is extensible” is a scenario. True or false? False. State the change, starting context and measurable effort or outcome. For example, adding a specified source through a documented adapter within three engineer-days, with existing tests still passing.

3. Organize a quality tree — 3. Organize a quality tree

A quality tree organizes the meaning of success. Its upper nodes are qualities such as performance, availability and maintainability. Lower nodes refine those qualities, and concrete scenarios sit at the leaves. The example’s larger tree also illustrates operational and reliability concerns; choose a tree suited to the system rather than treating one taxonomy as universal. Our compact worked tree has Performance → Search and Export; Availability → Worker loss; Maintainability → New source. The two performance leaves make a tradeoff inside that branch visible. Availability and Maintainability each have one leaf in this teaching example.

3. Organize a quality tree — Apply the idea: Organize a quality tree

Rank only children of the same parent. Ranking Availability against Performance is one conversation; ranking Search against Export is another. Do not mix a top-level quality with a scenario several levels below it. Record the rationale for each ordering and involve the people who bear the consequences. Check coverage before calculating. Missing a security requirement or representing latency with many near-duplicate scenarios can distort the decision. Mandatory requirements should be recorded explicitly. A high weighted total cannot justify violating a requirement that stakeholders have declared non-negotiable. Delay advocacy for particular solutions until the criteria and priorities are established. QODA borrows scenario thinking from ATAM, but the lecture presents it as a lightweight decision exercise, not as a complete execution of ATAM. If Performance has ten leaves and Availability has one, must Performance receive ten times the weight? No. The top-level allocation is determined by sibling priorities. Leaf weights subdivide their parent’s allocation. Carefully avoid redundant leaves that double-count the same concern.

4. Convert rankings to ROC weights — 4. Convert rankings to ROC weights

Rank-order-centroid weights translate an ordering into a reproducible set of numbers. Suppose Performance ranks first, Availability second, and Maintainability third. The bars show the resulting weights; they do not claim that we have directly measured stakeholder preferences. For three siblings, the first weight is one plus one half plus one third, divided by three. That is eleven eighteenths, approximately zero point six one one one. The second is one half plus one third, divided by three: five eighteenths. The last is one third divided by three: one ninth. The three weights sum to one. Compute this rule separately at each branching node. A two-child branch instead receives zero point seven five and zero point two five. If stakeholders disagree about the ordering, record that disagreement and test alternative rankings. Decimal precision does not remove the judgment that produced the ranking.

4. Convert rankings to ROC weights — Apply the idea: Convert rankings to ROC weights

For two siblings the weights are 0.75 and 0.25. Thus Search above Export receives 0.75 and 0.25 within Performance. A node with one child gives it weight 1. Compute weights independently at every branching node; do not reuse the three-child numbers for a two-child branch. The lecture uses stack rankings. If stakeholders cannot agree on an ordering, capture the disagreement and compare alternative rankings. Do not silently invent a tie-breaking convention or claim that the resulting decimal values remove subjective judgment. Are the ROC weights for three siblings 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2? No. They are 11/18, 5/18 and 1/9, approximately 0.6111, 0.2778 and 0.1111. Apply the stated formula rather than substituting intuitive percentages.

5. Multiply weights down each path — 5. Multiply weights down each path

The combined weight of a scenario is the product of all local weights on its root-to-leaf path. Do not add the weights. The parent allocates a share of the total, and each descendant allocates a share of that share. Using the worked tree: Search = (11/18) × (3/4) = 11/24 ≈ 0.4583. Export = (11/18) × (1/4) = 11/72 ≈ 0.1528. Worker loss = (5/18) × 1 ≈ 0.2778. New source = (1/9) × 1 ≈ 0.1111. The four leaf weights sum to one, and the two performance leaves sum to 11/18.

5. Multiply weights down each path — Apply the idea: Multiply weights down each path

A deeper tree works the same way. If a Performance → Latency branch has local weight 0.6111 and a particular latency scenario has local weight 0.75, multiply those by the top-level Performance weight as well. Omitting an intermediate level changes the meaning of the allocation. Useful checks are local sibling sums, complete path products and the total over all leaves. If the model includes branches with no assessed scenarios, disclose the uncovered weight. Do not silently renormalize the covered leaves and pretend the original decision criteria were fully evaluated. Search has a 0.6111 parent weight and a 0.75 local weight. Is its total weight 1.3611? No. Multiply: approximately 0.4583. Addition would not preserve the parent’s allocation and could produce a total above one.
