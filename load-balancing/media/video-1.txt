Failures and safe deployments

The archive runs four workers, each measured to handle 100 requests per second for a specified workload. At 280 requests per second, removing one worker leaves a nominal capacity of 300. That small margin may be inadequate once request costs vary or a dependency slows. A rolling deployment needs spare capacity as well as a sequence of replacement steps. Stop new assignments, allow existing work to drain within a deadline, deploy, and verify readiness before returning the worker. A failed health probe is evidence that the checked path did not respond acceptably; it is not proof of a process crash. Overload, network trouble or a dependency can produce the same observation. Failure thresholds reduce reactions to brief disturbances but delay removal, so some requests may fail before routing changes. Explain what clients may retry and with what limit. During a rolling update, old and new versions coexist, which makes API and storage compatibility part of deployment safety rather than an optional cleanup task.
