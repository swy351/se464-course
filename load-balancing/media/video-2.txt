DNS and global discovery

A resolver receives Region East with a 90-second TTL at time zero. At time 20, the authoritative policy switches new answers to Region West. Under the simplified ordinary-cache model, a lookup at time 50 can still receive East from the resolver’s cache; the answer becomes eligible for refresh at time 90. Updating the authority changes future answers, not every previously distributed copy at the same instant. Existing connections add another layer: a client may continue using an established connection without repeating DNS for every request. Multiple clients may also share one resolver, so a single cached answer can direct a large group. Geographic steering often estimates location from the resolver or partial client-network information, rather than knowing each user’s precise position. Treat DNS as a discovery mechanism with caching behavior. Combine it with regional load balancing and an explicit data recovery strategy; changing a name-to-address answer does not by itself make another region ready to accept writes.
