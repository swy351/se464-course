Design exercise: feeds, fan-out and locality
Part 1 • Synthetic narration

Start with normalized data and a read workload

Consider a hypothetical professional discussion service with tables for members, subscriptions and posts. A member following forty authors requests the newest twenty posts. A normalized design first finds the followed authors and retrieves their recent posts, then orders and limits the result. This can work well with suitable indexes and modest traffic. The architectural question is how much data and how many storage owners the request must touch as the workload changes. Now compare a member who follows four thousand authors spread across many partitions. The same logical query may require broader fan-out and merging, even though the returned page is still twenty posts. A small result does not imply a small amount of work. Measure feed-read frequency, author activity and partition fan-out before changing the model. Normalization makes canonical updates straightforward; it does not imply that every read is cheap. Conversely, the presence of a join alone does not prove the query is slow. The workload and access path provide the evidence.


Materialized feeds and asynchronous publication

In the hypothetical discussion service, one author has eight hundred followers. Materializing an entry in every follower's feed turns one canonical post into as many as eight hundred derived updates. Readers benefit because their recent feed entries are already grouped by their own identifier. At ten such posts per second, this illustrative case creates up to eight thousand feed updates per second before retries or other work. Faster reads have purchased additional write amplification. Queue the derived updates so the posting request can acknowledge durable acceptance without waiting for every destination. Then define a freshness target for readers and measure the oldest unfinished publication work. Workers can process bounded follower batches and use stable post identifiers to avoid duplicate entries after retries. Keep the canonical post distinct from feed references so that deletion or moderation has an explicit path through derived state. Asynchrony changes when the work happens and who waits; it does not remove the work or automatically make all feeds current.
